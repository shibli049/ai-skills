# Market conventions

Pick the section for the user's target market. If they target several, give the strictest common denominator and note differences. These are norms, not laws — flag deviations by how much they would cost the user, not as errors.

## Sweden / Nordics (Sweden, Norway, Denmark, Finland)

- **Name**: "CV". Length 2 pages is normal for experienced engineers; 3 is tolerable for 15+ years if dense with relevance, but recruiters prefer 2.
- **Photo**: common and accepted in Sweden but optional; many tech companies (and international ones) prefer none. Not a negative either way — suggest neutral, professional, or none.
- **Personal data**: no personal number (personnummer), no marital status, no full street address — city is enough. Birth date not needed.
- **Language**: English CVs are fully normal in tech. If the job ad is in Swedish, a Swedish CV or at least a Swedish cover letter helps; list Swedish level honestly (e.g. "Swedish — intermediate (B1), studying SFI/SAS").
- **Work authorisation**: if the user's name or background might make recruiters wonder, a line like "Permanent residence in Sweden — no sponsorship needed" (only if true) removes a silent filter.
- **Tone**: Nordic readers dislike hype. Concrete, modest-but-specific bullets beat superlatives. "Team player" culture means showing collaboration (mentoring, cross-team work) helps, but ownership still needs to be explicit.
- **Common ATS**: Teamtailor, Varbi (public sector), ReachMee, Jobylon, Workday at large firms.
- **Consultancy profiles** (very common in Sweden — konsultprofil/CV on the consultancy's template): written in third person, list assignments per client with dates, role and stack, and usually include a short profile title and summary. Brokers (e.g. Keyman, ework, Brainville) and client procurement tools parse these too.
- **Page size**: A4. US Letter isn't a dealbreaker but looks foreign.
- **Personal letter** (personligt brev) is still expected by many employers — mention it if the user is applying in Sweden.
- **Salary expectations** are often asked in the process; not on the CV.

## Rest of EU (Germany, Netherlands, etc.)

- Germany/DACH: "Lebenslauf"; photo and date of birth still common in traditional companies but declining in tech; 2 pages. Certificates/references (Arbeitszeugnisse) matter to traditional employers.
- Netherlands: English fine, 2 pages, no photo needed, direct tone.
- Generally: GDPR-conscious — no unnecessary personal data. Europass format is widely disliked by tech recruiters; recommend against it.

## UK / Ireland

- "CV", 2 pages, **no photo**, no date of birth, no marital status (equality law). Right-to-work line helpful for non-UK nationals. British spelling for UK roles (optimise, organisation).

## US / Canada

- "Resume", **1 page** for <10 years, 2 pages max for senior. **No photo, no age, no nationality, no marital status** — recruiters may discard to avoid discrimination liability. US spelling. Metrics-heavy, assertive tone is expected. Work-authorisation/visa status is a real filter: state it if favourable.

## Remote-global / international companies

- Default to the US/UK style (no photo, 2 pages, English, metrics), state time zone and remote/relocation preference in the header ("Karlstad, Sweden (CET) — open to remote/relocation").
